Project Thesis

A report does not have to feel administrative — with the right voiceover, pacing, and edit, it can become a story people can follow, feel, and remember.

The Green Print metaphor became the narrative device that helped the video move from activity recap into meaning-making.

Green Print is a motion project where the main design challenge was not the report itself, but how to translate a written annual report into a video people would actually follow, feel, and remember.

The source material covered a full year of Mangena Maake Mokone Memorial Conference YPD activities, but I treated those details as the foundation, not the centre of the project page. The creative task was to shape that information into a voiceover-led visual narrative with a clear beginning, development, reflection, and closing thought.

The video uses a Vox-style inspired report structure: narration carries the argument, visuals support the point, and the edit moves between context, montage, and meaning. The concept of a Green Print became the thread that tied the report together, reframing the year as something organic, faith-led, and built through movement rather than rigid planning.

My focus was on editing rhythm, voiceover delivery, sequencing, pacing, and emotional build. Instead of allowing the report to feel like a compressed list of events, the final video turns it into a reflective motion piece about growth, collaboration, resilience, and faith-led leadership.
