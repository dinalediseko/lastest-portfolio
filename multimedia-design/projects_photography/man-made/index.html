Project Thesis

Man-made things often perform permanence, but texture tells the truth.

This series looks at the quiet instability of built objects — how weather, use, neglect, and nature expose the limits of what people construct.

Man-Made is an observational photography project focused on the fragility of objects, structures, and surfaces created by people. The images look at the built environment not as something fixed, but as something constantly being worn down, altered, and exposed.

The series moves between signage, architecture, walls, brickwork, sculptural objects, and weathered surfaces. Each frame highlights the tension between human intention and material weakness — things made to direct, protect, decorate, or last begin to show cracks, marks, fading, rust, peeling paint, and imperfect edges.

The photographs are intentionally unedited. I wanted the work to keep its raw daylight, natural colour shifts, and visible imperfections instead of making the scenes feel overly polished. That choice supports the concept: the fragility is not hidden, corrected, or beautified. It is allowed to remain visible.

At its core, this project is about looking closely at the ordinary built world. It reflects my interest in how photography can reveal the quiet vulnerability of things we usually treat as stable, useful, or permanent.

Frame notes

Surface, structure, and weathering carry the story.

Surface

Texture becomes the main subject: scratches, chipped paint, brick patterns, stains, and uneven finishes reveal the physical life of each object.

Structure

The compositions use buildings, signs, posts, and walls to show how constructed forms can still feel vulnerable, temporary, and exposed.

Weathering

Natural light, sky, trees, and plants sit beside the built objects, reminding the viewer that nature continues to press against what humans make.
