Project Thesis

Ordinary spaces can become cinematic when movement gives them rhythm.

The campus was not designed as a BMX track, but through motion, framing, and youth expression, it begins to feel like one.

Ride the Campus Like a BMX Track is a photography project focused on movement, youth culture, and the way familiar environments can be transformed through personal expression.

The series follows a BMX rider moving through the Tshwane University of Technology Arts Campus, a space that is not necessarily designed for performance, but becomes shaped by the way he interacts with it. Curbs, walkways, parking areas, shadows, and open pockets of space become part of the visual language, turning the campus into something more playful, cinematic, and alive.

The creative direction leans into a slightly moody, documentary-inspired style. I wanted the images to feel unpolished in the right way — not overly staged, but still intentional. The darker tones, soft blur, and natural framing help create a sense of movement while keeping the focus on atmosphere, rhythm, and the rider's presence within the space.

At its core, this project is about seeing potential in ordinary surroundings. It reflects my interest in photography that does not only document a subject, but reframes the environment around them.

Frame notes

The series is built through motion, environment, and mood.

Instead of adding another gallery, this section breaks down the visual choices that make the selected frames feel cinematic and alive.

Motion

Soft blur, body angle, and wheel movement keep the photographs from feeling frozen. The rider’s movement becomes the energy of the frame.

Environment

The campus architecture is treated as part of the composition. Curbs, walkways, shadows, and empty pockets of space become visual obstacles and rhythm markers.

Mood

The darker edit and natural framing give the project a documentary feeling — unpolished enough to feel real, but intentional enough to feel cinematic.
