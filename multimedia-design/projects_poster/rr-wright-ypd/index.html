RR Wright YPD Identity & T-Shirt Design

An identity-led project built through logo reconstruction, monogram design, and concept application.

About the Project

RR Wright YPD was a local circuit's apparel design project that began with the need to create a custom t-shirt system rooted in both identity and symbolism. One of the first design challenges was that the original logo asset provided by the client was not production-ready, as it still contained a Canva watermark. This meant the project first required a clean logo vectorisation and rebuild process before any further design development could begin.

From there, I developed a stronger supporting visual language for the brand by creating a custom RR Wright YPD monogram, which was then expanded into a repeat pattern system to give the apparel more character, cohesion, and ownability. This pattern became a key design accent across the final shirt concepts, including the “Child of God” variation, where it was introduced.

The client also shared a hand-drawn concept sketch showing their preferred direction for the shirt — particularly the use of the two Rs and a tick mark as a visual play on the word “Wright” / “Right”. I used this sketch as the conceptual foundation for the final design, refining it into a more balanced and wearable solution. While the original intention included a fully patterned sleeve, I made the decision to simplify the composition to avoid visual overload, especially since one of the Rs already incorporated the monogram pattern. This allowed the second R to remain a solid green for contrast and clarity, creating a cleaner hierarchy.

The final result balanced identity design, apparel application, and visual problem-solving, turning an initial rough concept into a more cohesive and production-conscious design system that could function both as ministry wear and as a recognisable visual statement.

“This project reflects the kind of work I do in service spaces — building visuals that don’t just look good, but help people feel invited, informed, and part of something bigger.”
