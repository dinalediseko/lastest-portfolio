The Art of What Could Be — Concept Phase

ViaTown.

A crowdsourced public transport tracking platform for South Africa — combining official schedules with commuter-driven real-time updates. Because most transit connects in town.

Project Thesis

The Core Idea

South Africans already crowdsource load shedding. Why not public transport?

Movement is information.

ViaTown is not a new idea — it's an adaptation of one that already works. EskomSePush proved that when official information is fragmented, communities will collectively fill the gap if given the right platform. This concept applies that same model to the daily unpredictability of buses, trains, and taxis.

The problem is urgent and lived. Tshwane Bus Services communicates via X (Twitter). Google Maps covers the Gautrain but not the minibus taxis, PRASA routes, or municipal buses most South Africans rely on. Commuters lose time, money, and dignity — not because they don't want to plan, but because the tools don't exist.

The bold version asks: what if the Gautrain ran 24 hours? Core lines, reduced frequency, connecting night-shift workers, airport travellers, and a city that no longer sleeps. Not every route, not every stop — but enough to prove that world-class infrastructure can meet a city's full rhythm.

What makes this different from Google Maps? Google Maps is built for private vehicle navigation first. Its public transport coverage in South Africa is static, incomplete, and disconnected from the lived realities of minibus taxis and municipal services. ViaTown would be built by commuters, for commuters — zero-rated, community-verified, and designed around how South Africans actually move.

And the taxi industry? This platform would never track operational data — no driver identities, no registration details, no route profitability. Only commuter-relevant information: estimated time of arrival, passenger capacity, and cost of the ride. By centring the commuter experience, the platform supports rather than threatens the industry's role in urban mobility.

Conceptual Pillars

Three principles the concept doesn't compromise on.

Community Over Infrastructure

The platform is not built on official APIs alone. It's built on commuters reporting what they see, know, and experience — verified by the community, not just the institution.

Accessibility as Principle

Zero-rated data, minimal battery consumption, and an interface designed for diverse digital literacy levels. If it doesn't work for the commuter at the rank, it doesn't work.

The Gautrain Dream

The Gautrain has shown what world-class public transport can look like in South Africa. This concept dares to ask what it would take to make that the baseline, not the exception — including 24-hour operations.
